faqGroups:
  - title: "Before You Start"
    items:
      - question: "When should I bring in an architecture firm?"
        answer: "Ideally before you buy a building or hire a contractor. At this early stage, many things can still be clarified: what's legally possible, roughly what it will cost, and where the risks lie. Later on, decisions have often already been made and can only be changed at a high cost."
      - question: "What happens in the first conversation?"
        answer: "You tell us what you have in mind, and we ask about the building, the budget and the timeframe. Afterwards, we tell you honestly whether we're the right firm for it and what the sensible next steps would be. How long the initial consultation takes depends on the project."
      - question: "What should I bring to the first appointment?"
        answer: "Anything you have on the building: plans, old building permits, photos, a land register extract or the property listing. If you don't have any of that, it's not a problem. Existing records can often be viewed by requesting file access from the relevant building authority."
      - question: "Do you also take on new-build projects, or only work on existing buildings?"
        answer: "Yes, we plan new builds too. Our focus, however, is on building within existing structures – conversion, renovation and change of use. For new builds, we take on the full planning and site supervision, or individual stages, such as tendering and awarding the construction contracts. The experience from working with existing buildings also helps with new builds: we pay early attention to building law, costs and a design that's easy to implement later on."
  - title: "Permits and Building Law"
    items:
      - question: "Do I need a building permit for my conversion?"
        answer: "That depends on exactly what you want to change. Smaller interior works are often permit-free. For interventions in the structural system, extensions, additional storeys or a change of use, a permit is generally required. Important: even a permit-free project still has to comply with building regulations. We check that for your specific case."
      - question: "What is a change of use, and why is it so involved?"
        answer: "A change of use applies when a room or building is to be used differently going forward – for example, a shop becoming an apartment, or a basement becoming an event space. Different requirements often apply to the new use: fire protection, escape routes, parking spaces, sound insulation or accessibility. That's why a building application is usually needed, even if little changes structurally."
      - question: "How long does a building application take?"
        answer: "That varies a great deal depending on the authority and the project. Realistically, plan for several months, and longer for more complex cases. A complete, well-coordinated application noticeably shortens the process, since follow-up requests cost the most time."
      - question: "Does the same building law apply across the whole Rhine-Main region?"
        answer: "No. The Rhine-Main region spans Hesse, Rhineland-Palatinate and Bavaria, and each state has its own building code. On top of that come municipal bylaws, for example on parking spaces or design. What applies in Frankfurt can be regulated differently in Mainz or Aschaffenburg."
  - title: "Costs and Fees"
    items:
      - question: "How is the architect's fee calculated?"
        answer: "The basis is usually the HOAI, Germany's fee schedule for architects and engineers. Since 2021, fees have been freely negotiable, and the HOAI values now serve only as a guide. For consultations and smaller services, we bill based on time spent. Whatever applies to your project, we put in writing before we begin."
      - question: "What will my conversion cost?"
        answer: "A reliable figure only becomes possible once the existing building is known and what you want to do is settled. Existing buildings often hold surprises – damp, hazardous materials, or a structure that doesn't match the records. That's why we work with cost estimates that get more precise with every planning phase, and we build in a buffer for the unforeseen."
      - question: "Are there grants available for renovation?"
        answer: "For energy-efficient renovation, there are often funding programmes from the federal government, the states or local authorities. The conditions change regularly, and many programmes have to be applied for before you place an order. So clarify the funding question before commissioning the work, not after. From 2027, we'll offer energy consulting and funding advice, planning your renovation from a single source, including the right funding. You can already book appointments for 2027 with us now."
  - title: "Working Together"
    items:
      - question: "Do I have to commission you for the whole project?"
        answer: "No. You can also commission us for individual steps, for example just a feasibility study, just the building application, or just tendering and awarding contracts. We discuss with you what scope makes sense for your project."
      - question: "What is a feasibility study, and do I need one?"
        answer: "A feasibility study clarifies, before major investment, what's possible on a plot or in a building – legally, spatially and economically. It's particularly worthwhile before a purchase or before a change of use. For comparatively little effort, you get a solid basis for your decision."
